**Title:** Racialized Homelessness: A Review of Historical and Contemporary Causes of Racial Disparities in Homelessness  
**Author:** Matthew Z. Fowle  
**Journal:** Housing Policy Debate  
**Publication Year:** 2022  
**Volume/Issue/Pages:** 32(6), 940–967  
**DOI:** 10.1080/10511482.2022.2026995  
**Link:** https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10511482.2022.2026995  

#### Abstract (Summary)

This article synthesizes historical and contemporary scholarship to explain why homelessness in the United States is disproportionately concentrated among Black, Latinx, and Native American populations. Rather than treating homelessness as race-neutral or reducible to individual deficits, the review frames homelessness as a racialized outcome produced through structural racism operating across housing, economic, political, and social institutions. The article argues that racial disparities in homelessness are closely tied to long-standing patterns of housing discrimination, residential segregation, economic inequality, and unequal treatment within homelessness response systems. The review concludes that meaningful reductions in homelessness disparities require structural reforms that confront these interconnected institutional systems.

#### Introduction

Despite representing a smaller proportion of the overall U.S. population, Black, Latinx, and Native American individuals are disproportionately represented among people experiencing homelessness. The article examines why these racial disparities persist and argues that explanations focused only on poverty, mental health, or individual behavior are insufficient. Instead, the review situates homelessness within broader historical and sociological processes involving racial exclusion, segregation, and institutional inequality.

#### Background

Previous homelessness research has often emphasized factors such as housing affordability, unemployment, mental illness, and service availability. However, this article argues that less attention has been given to how racial stratification shapes exposure to these risks. Drawing from sociological and historical scholarship, the review traces how discriminatory housing and economic systems have constrained wealth accumulation, residential stability, and access to safe housing for communities of color across generations.

#### Method

This article uses a sociological literature review approach to synthesize historical and contemporary research on racial disparities in homelessness. The author reviewed scholarship from sociology, housing studies, economics, public policy, and homelessness research to identify recurring structural patterns associated with racialized homelessness. The review draws on demographic homelessness statistics, historical housing policy analyses, segregation research, eviction studies, housing discrimination research, and studies of homeless service systems. Rather than relying on a single dataset or experiment, the article compares findings across multiple studies to examine how institutional systems interact to shape homelessness outcomes over time.

#### Results / Findings

The reviewed research consistently found that homelessness disproportionately affects Black, Native American, and Latinx populations in the United States. Several studies cited in the article reported that Black Americans make up approximately 13% of the U.S. population but nearly 40% of the homeless population nationally. Research reviewed in the article also found that Black individuals are more likely to experience unsheltered homelessness and longer durations of homelessness than White individuals.

Across the reviewed scholarship, racial disparities persisted even when controlling for poverty, suggesting that economic hardship alone does not fully explain homelessness disparities. The review repeatedly identified structural factors such as residential segregation, discriminatory lending and rental practices, eviction disparities, unequal access to housing wealth, and disproportionate criminal legal system involvement as major contributors to housing instability among communities of color.

Historical analyses reviewed in the article connected present-day homelessness disparities to long-term institutional processes such as redlining, racially restrictive covenants, urban renewal displacement, discriminatory welfare policies, and exclusion from wealth-building housing opportunities.

#### Discussion

The article argues that homelessness cannot be fully understood through individual-level explanations alone because racial inequality is embedded across multiple institutional systems. From a sociological perspective, homelessness is viewed as a structural outcome shaped by historical and contemporary systems of power, inequality, and exclusion. The review suggests that homelessness policies focused only on shelters or emergency services are unlikely to eliminate racial disparities unless broader inequalities in housing access, wealth accumulation, criminal legal exposure, and social policy are also addressed.

#### Conclusion

The article concludes that homelessness in the United States is deeply shaped by structural racism operating across housing, economic, and political systems. Reducing racial disparities in homelessness requires structural reforms such as enforcing fair housing protections, reducing discriminatory housing practices, expanding affordable housing access, addressing racial wealth gaps, and reforming institutional systems that contribute to unequal housing vulnerability.
