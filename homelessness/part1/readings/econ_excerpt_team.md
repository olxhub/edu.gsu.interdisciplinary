#### Results

**Results show strong empirical support for both hypotheses. States with higher levels of entrepreneurial activity exhibit lower homelessness rates. Similarly, greater labor market freedom is associated with lower levels of homelessness.** Granger causality tests indicate that changes in entrepreneurial activity and labor market freedom precede changes in homelessness rates, suggesting directional influence rather than simple correlation.

#### Discussion

**The findings indicate that homelessness is associated not only with housing markets and social policy, but also with broader institutional economic conditions.** By introducing measures of entrepreneurial activity and labor market freedom, the study broadens structural explanations of homelessness to include economic opportunity structures and regulatory environments.

#### Conclusion

**Homelessness rates are negatively associated with entrepreneurial activity and labor market freedom at the state level. Institutional economic design appears to play a role in shaping homelessness outcomes. Policies that promote entrepreneurship and labor market flexibility may complement housing and welfare interventions in efforts to reduce homelessness.**
