#### Results

**Greater access to cash assistance through TANF is associated with significantly lower rates of family homelessness.** Increases in the accessibility of TANF cash benefits correspond with measurable declines in family homelessness rates. The negative association between TANF access and homelessness is particularly pronounced in majority–Black and Native American school districts. In contrast, greater access to SNAP benefits does not show a consistent relationship with reduced homelessness, and increased generosity of the EITC yields inconclusive effects on homelessness levels.

#### Discussion

**These findings suggest that direct cash assistance may play a distinctive role in reducing family homelessness, beyond what in-kind (SNAP) or conditional wage subsidy (EITC) programs achieve.** The study highlights how policy retrenchment — particularly declines in TANF accessibility — may help explain recent trends in homelessness among families with children. It also underscores the importance of considering racial and ethnic disparities in program effects.

#### Conclusion

Family and student homelessness in the U.S. is associated with the accessibility of income support policies. **Greater access to cash assistance through TANF is linked to lower homelessness rates, suggesting that policy design within the welfare state influences housing stability.** SNAP and EITC do not show clear protective associations in this study. Integrating income support policy into homelessness research provides a broader understanding of structural determinants of housing insecurity.
