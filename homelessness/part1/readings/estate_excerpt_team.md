#### Results

**The literature reviewed identified consistent evidence that climate-related environmental hazards increasingly shape housing markets and housing stability.** Across multiple studies, properties exposed to flood risk experienced measurable declines in housing values once environmental risks became publicly visible through updated flood maps, disaster exposure, or disclosure policies. For example, one study reviewed found that homes newly assigned to flood zones experienced approximately an 11% decrease in property prices following changes in flood risk mapping. Other studies reported that repeated flood exposure contributed to declining housing demand and lower long-term property values in high-risk areas.

Research on hurricanes, severe storms, and wildfire exposure similarly found disruptions to local housing markets, increased insurance costs, and longer-term displacement pressures for affected populations. Several studies reviewed reported increased mortgage delinquency risks and worsening financial outcomes among households affected by flooding and climate-related disasters. One study discussed in the review found that, outside protected floodplain areas, flood exposure was associated with a 20% increase in bankruptcies and a 13% increase in severe debt delinquency among credit-constrained homeowners.

The review also found evidence that climate risks increasingly influence migration and residential decision-making. Households with greater financial resources were often more capable of relocating away from environmentally vulnerable areas, while lower-income populations experienced greater difficulty recovering from environmental disasters or relocating to safer housing markets. As a result, climate-related housing instability disproportionately affected economically vulnerable populations.

**Across the literature, environmental risks were increasingly found to shape housing affordability, insurance availability, lending decisions, migration patterns, and long-term residential stability. The authors argue that climate change is becoming an increasingly important factor in understanding housing precarity and displacement.**

#### Discussion

**The findings suggest that climate change affects housing systems not only through direct environmental destruction, but also through economic and market processes that shape housing stability.** Environmental hazards may alter where people can afford to live, increase displacement pressures, and intensify housing precarity for already vulnerable populations. **The article argues that climate vulnerability should be considered an increasingly important factor in housing stability and homelessness prevention efforts.**

#### Conclusion

**Climate-related environmental hazards such as flooding, hurricanes, wildfires, and sea level rise increasingly shape housing markets, affordability, displacement, and housing stability. The growing interaction between climate systems and housing markets highlights the importance of understanding homelessness and housing precarity not only as economic or social issues, but also as problems shaped by environmental risk and climate vulnerability.**
